# Patients arrive unprepared because the reminder leaves out the preparation

Some appointments need the patient to do something beforehand: no food for 8 hours, bring the previous X-rays, arrive with a full bladder. We enter that on the appointment. Too often the reminder the patient receives doesn't mention it.

Example. Marta had a blood test booked on 4 Oct at 09:30, with the preparation "no food for 8 hours before". The message she received was:

> Hi Marta, a reminder of your appointment at Riverside Clinic on 4 Oct at 09:30. Questions? Call 020 7946 0131.

She had breakfast, and we had to rebook her. That is our standard wording, which never mentions preparation. But I have also seen friendlier, individually written reminders that skip the instruction or get it wrong ("please don't eat for 6 hours").

What I want: when an appointment has a preparation instruction, the patient always receives it, in our exact words, whichever wording the rest of the reminder uses. If that makes the reminder longer than one text message, so be it: a long reminder costs us less than an empty slot. Appointments with no preparation keep working as they do today.
