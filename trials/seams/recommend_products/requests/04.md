# The reason under a recommended product sometimes states a price or rating that isn't the real one

I manage the catalogue for the shop. The "Recommended for your search" strip shows each product with its price and rating and, underneath, a short line saying why it suits the search. Now and then that line contradicts the figures printed right above it.

Two examples from this month. A search for "wireless headphones" recommended the Aria wireless headphones, shown at 129.00, with the reason "Great sound for under 100". A search for "usb-c cable" recommended the 1 m cable, rated 4.4, with the reason "Our top-rated cable", while the 2 m cable in the same strip is rated 4.6.

Customers notice. We have had two emails asking us to honour the "under 100" price, and our customer service team had to explain that the line was wrong. Beyond the awkwardness, a stated price that differs from the one charged is the kind of thing that gets a shop into trouble with consumer protection rules.

I want shoppers to be able to rely on that line: whatever it says about a product's price, rating or standing against the other products should agree with what the shop actually shows for it. The Aria should never appear with a reason that promises a price it doesn't have.
