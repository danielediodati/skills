# Routine requests from enterprise customers show up as urgent, next to real emergencies

I'm the duty lead on the support desk. On our desk "urgent" means something is broken for a customer right now: it carries a one-hour answer, and it pages whoever is on call. Lately a large part of the urgent list is nothing of the kind.

Last Wednesday an enterprise customer sent a ticket with the subject "Copy of August invoice". Their accounts team needed it for their month-end close on Friday. It is a fair request and it deserved a prompt answer. It arrived marked urgent, answer within one hour, and it paged me at 6:40 in the morning.

Twenty minutes later a customer on the Pro plan reported that their checkout page was failing for every one of their shoppers. That ticket was also marked urgent, also one hour. In the list the two looked exactly alike. The agent on shift took them in the order they came in and sent the invoice copy first.

This is now the pattern. Enterprise tickets that would be "high" from anyone else land in urgent, so on most mornings more than half of the urgent list is invoice copies, how-to questions and requests to add a user. My agents have learned that urgent usually isn't, and they have stopped dropping everything for it. We missed the one-hour answer on four real outages last month.

Our enterprise contracts promise a faster answer, and I want to keep that promise. But I also want "urgent" to mean an emergency again, so that the people on call can trust it. The invoice request should have reached us ahead of ordinary tickets without looking the same as a checkout that is down.
